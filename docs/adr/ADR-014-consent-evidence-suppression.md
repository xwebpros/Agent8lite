# ADR-014 — Native consent evidence and suppression

Accepted for bounded local implementation on 2026-10-10 after coordinator and test-owner review. Normative requirements: MOD-D consent provenance and archive boundaries, MOD-E-004/007, INV-03/07, frozen ConsentWrite and consent.changed event. Contracts and the forward migration precede consumers; implementation and acceptance remain separate gates. This is a local synthetic mechanism, with no legal policy approval or live delivery claim.

## Contract and ownership

Keep `POST /v1/orgs/{orgId}/consent-events` unchanged: ConsentWrite, Idempotency-Key, CSRF, verified membership, `consent.write` organization permission, Job202. Contact omits channel IDs and there is no production channel-read contract. Add reviewed loopback-only `/dev/consent-channels/:contactId` GET and `/dev/consent-evidence` POST with closed internal schemas for synthetic UI/tests. Production channel readback and evidence collection remain a contract gate.

`NativeConsentPort` is bound to the caller's verified tenant transaction, as capture is. It owns consent_event/state/evidence and suppression writes. `ConsentChannelPort`, implemented by CRM, resolves the requested channel through current contact read/write scope, active channel and unarchived contact. Normalized destinations travel privately through that port, never through logs/events. Record requires both contact read and write access. The eligibility reader requires current contact read access; future workers receive only reviewed source grants when those consumers exist.

Withdrawal-only source resolution is the explicit exception to active/unarchived source status: `access: withdraw` may resolve a preserved inactive channel or archived contact while applying the same current contact ownership/read/write scope and consent permission. It grants no ordinary reads, new evidence, grants or send eligibility. A withdrawal from such a source still suppresses its preserved normalized destination on other contacts. The initial dev channel selector shows only active/unarchived sources; production/archive source selection remains a UI/read-contract gap, while a caller holding an authorized preserved channel ID can submit the frozen withdrawal command.

Record receives a required durable `ConsentCommandIdentity {producer, key}` from its trusted caller, separate from the frozen body. The service owns idempotence under verified tenant/actor plus that server-selected namespace, canonical body hash and command key, and saves the requester Job response in the same transaction. A larger form transaction can supply a stable entry/purpose command key without creating independent transactions or duplicate withdrawals. The HTTP adapter passes the original Idempotency-Key under the fixed staff namespace and does not wrap the command in a second idempotence layer. Producer is never accepted from the public request body.

Grants require enabled CRM entitlement and trusted evidence. Withdrawals and withdrawal replay remain possible when commercial entitlement is suspended; scope, active membership and permission checks still apply. Grant replay rechecks current entitlement and source authorization before returning the historical Job. Every replay preserves later withdrawals; it never re-executes the original grant. A historical succeeded Job means the ledger command committed, not that current marketing eligibility or delivery succeeded.

## Trusted synthetic evidence

No evidenceRef string, URL, arbitrary policyVersion, contact import, ordinary inquiry or checkbox directly proves a grant. Evidence resolution never fetches a URL. Synthetic evidence is issued only by the gated local endpoint after verified source read/write access, `consent.write`, CSRF and CRM capability. Server selects `synthetic-communication-v1`, stamps synthetic provenance, and stores an immutable UUID reference bound to tenant/channel/purpose/action/policy, current consent-state revision and source revisions, with fifteen-minute expiry. Published grant accepts only `synthetic-evidence:<uuid>` resolved to that record. Outside explicitly enabled development/test mode, synthetic evidence is rejected. Unknown production evidence/policy denies grant.

The proof also binds the current initiating member explicitly, and the evidence resolver requires that member to equal the verified context member. Issuance requires its own durable Idempotency-Key and canonical request identity. Exact issuance replay returns the same original proof after source/permission reauthorization; expired or consumed proofs remain expired or consumed and are never renewed by replay. Server derives creation/expiry from one database clock value, with a maximum fifteen-minute interval. That interval limits proof consumption, not the lifetime of a successfully recorded grant: an accepted grant does not automatically opt out after fifteen minutes. Future policy expiry requires its own approved policy semantics.

The dev channel response uses generic labels such as Email channel 1, never destination values, contact names, proof IDs or policy prose. Typed dev request/response exports live once in the shared proposal, and both API and frontend import them; no consumer invents its own local contract.

Email marketing and initial listing alerts require an email channel; SMS marketing requires a phone channel. Phone-based listing alerts remain denied pending explicit purpose/policy rights. Source revisions, proof scope, expiry and state revision must all match when recording a grant. A proof issued before a withdrawal is stale after the withdrawal increments state. One proof can back one ledger grant; a different command key cannot reuse it. Exact idempotent replay returns the original committed Job after current authorization and never generates another event.

Withdrawals accept an opaque `staff-withdrawal:<uuid>` trace reference and a bounded policy-version identifier; neither is treated as proof of a grant. The verified initiating member and current channel ownership establish staff authority to record a withdrawal. Unknown policy wording does not block an opt-out or enable a send.

## Atomic ordering and suppression

Take a transaction-local advisory lock keyed by a SHA-256 hash of tenant/channel-kind/normalized-destination/purpose before state/proof reads or writes. No raw destination is used in a logged lock key. State row_version is the next ledger sequence; immutable consent_event records that sequence, initiating member and optional evidence UUID. Append event, update state, update destination/purpose suppression, create requester-scoped succeeded Job, audit and frozen consent.changed outbox envelope atomically. No PII, policy prose or evidence token enters audit/event payloads.

Deterministic source/lock order: obtain an unlocked scoped source reference only to derive the opaque destination key, acquire exclusive destination advisory lock (shared for eligibility reads), re-resolve and lock source contact then channel in that order, verify the original destination/source versions still match, then lock state and existing suppression. Any source change during lock acquisition conflicts instead of silently moving the command to another destination. Source locks remain until the caller's transaction commits. Future SELECT-only worker readers require a separately reviewed lock service/grant design before consuming this port.

CRM source locking also holds the current active lead rows and the office-membership rows used by any team scope decision, with deterministic row ordering, then rechecks scope. Existing member office changes take the organization update lock while verified requests hold its share lock. Forward0011 additionally grants runtime UPDATE on only office_member.office_id so PostgreSQL permits FOR SHARE on those scope rows; it does not grant worker access, introduce a public office mutation contract, or permit consent to write CRM tables. Deleted office links and changed ownership cannot authorize a command using an earlier unlocked observation.

A withdrawal sets state withdrawn and durable destination/purpose suppression before commit. Suppression applies to equivalent normalized destinations on other contacts. A fresh verified grant may release only a consent-withdrawal suppression, only after checking that no other channel for the same destination/purpose has an unresolved withdrawal. It cannot release bounce, complaint, unknown or other provider suppression reasons. Never merge contacts or consent simply because they share a phone/email. Ledger history and proof references remain immutable; suppression is a conservative projection and is never deleted by this slice.

Shared-destination unresolved withdrawals remain blocking after the original contact is archived or its channel becomes inactive. That projection scans preserved consent/channel identity history without excluding archived contacts or inactive channels. It returns only a blocking reason, never another contact's identity. No archive, deactivation or contact-name change clears a suppression.

`readEligibility` returns only state, safe reasons, consentEligible and an opaque revision hash bound to actor/tenant/purpose/channel/current permissions/source revisions/state/proof/suppression revisions and validity. It takes the corresponding shared advisory lock. `assertCurrent` re-resolves current source/grants and state, rejecting a stale revision or blocked consent. This is a consent reader, not a complete send authorizer. Future sending must separately recheck entitlement, archive/recipient state, sender/provider readiness, quiet hours, purpose policy, native action generation and unknown acceptance. This slice makes no transport call and does not claim to cancel schedules that do not exist yet; a future marketing cancellation port/event consumer is required.

`assertCurrent` ignores caller-supplied consentEligible, state and reasonCodes. It reloads current source authorization and persisted state, recomputes the revision and decision, and compares only the opaque prior revision as a stale-state guard. An attacker or mistaken caller cannot turn unknown/withdrawn consent into an allowed result by editing the snapshot. An already consumed and recorded proof is verified through immutable ledger provenance; its consumption deadline does not later expire an otherwise current accepted grant.

Readers constructed outside explicitly enabled synthetic development/test mode deny previously recorded synthetic grants as well as new grants. Persisted granted state alone never approves production eligibility. Current mode, evidence provenance and current policy approval participate in the snapshot revision; removal of that approval produces a safe blocking reason. Reusing a synthetic database under production configuration cannot enable sends. There is no production evidence resolver in this slice.

## Migration proposal

Forward0010 adds immutable synthetic evidence, mutable tenant/channel/purpose state, and consent_event sequence/member/evidence columns. Existing events retain their original evidence_ref and ordering; unverified legacy grants remain unknown, and any legacy withdrawal remains suppressing until a new approved proof is verified. Composite tenant FKs and FORCE RLS apply. Runtime receives SELECT/INSERT for consent ledger/evidence/state/suppression, UPDATE only reviewed state/suppression projection columns, no ledger/evidence UPDATE/DELETE, no suppression DELETE. Forward0011 permits the bounded office source lock described above. No worker grants until a reviewed dispatch consumer exists.

## Acceptance

Prove foreign/out-of-scope channels denied; inactive/archived sources deny evidence/grants/eligibility but permit a current-scope withdrawal; wrong purpose/channel/policy/proof tenant or source revision denied; expired/stale/consumed evidence denied; grant-withdraw race never revives a stale grant; withdrawal survives disabled capability; same-key retries create one ledger/event/job and return no unauthorized cached result; shared destination cannot clear another contact's withdrawal even after archive/deactivation; independent suppression remains blocking; real-role missing-context/pool reuse and ledger immutability hold; eligibility snapshot becomes stale after withdrawal/source/grant changes. UI labels every proof/grant synthetic and never claims successful delivery.
