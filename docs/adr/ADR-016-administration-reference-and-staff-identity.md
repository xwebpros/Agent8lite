# ADR-016 — Administration references and synthetic staff identity clients

Accepted bounded internal seams on 2026-10-10. This does not enable production staff authentication, support access or provisioning.

CMS references a business profile through the administration-owned `CmsProfileReferencePort` in `packages/contracts/internal/profile-reference-port.ts`. The factory in `apps/api/src/admin-profile-reference.ts` uses the invoking PoolClient and verified tenant context, requires a current organization CMS grant and websites capability, returns only profile/organization IDs, and holds a SHARE row lock until caller commit. Missing/foreign references deny without exposing profile fields. The current business-profile schema has no archive state; future archive semantics require an explicit administration schema/service change and must not be inferred from tenant JSON. The CMS service separately checks the specific operation permission. No nested transaction or cross-module write is introduced.

The mock OIDC provider defaults to owner/agent subjects. A separate staff client may supply a closed, detached server-owned allowlist containing staff-administrator/staff-support. Empty, duplicate, invented or mixed member/staff families deny at configuration; clients reject the other family's subjects and audience. Existing PKCE, one-use code, nonce, issuer/signature and expiry validation remains required. Browser actor claims never establish a staff identity. A verified token alone does not establish staff permission: the forthcoming separate issuer/control pools must resolve current principals and opaque sessions under real-role policies before accepting staff commands.

Executed evidence: sixteen identity protocol tests, plus four isolated real-runtime-role administration reference tests covering same-tenant projection, missing/foreign references, capability/scope denial, row locking through rollback and missing context after pool reuse. External IdP callback and production identity acceptance remain unverified.

Follow-up executed evidence: the capability guard uses `clock_timestamp()` and the profile resolver checks again after obtaining its source lock. A fifth real-role test waits past capability expiry on a held profile lock and proves denial. The profile/billing/consent regression batch passed31/31. Source-owning services must recheck after any later blocking locks; a SHARE lock on capability state cannot prevent passage of time.
